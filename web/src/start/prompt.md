## Task: Build a Minimum Builder Season Landing Page

Create a simple, polished landing page for **Builder Season** within the existing website/project.

Before implementing it, inspect the existing repository and follow its established framework, components, styles, build process, and conventions. Do not introduce a new framework or unnecessary dependencies.

### Purpose

We need a minimum public page that we can share with prospective participants, partners, and sponsors while an upcoming Builder Season is still being organized.

This is **not** the complete event website and should not contain detailed rules, finalized dates, prizes, schedules, project registration, dashboards, or other infrastructure.

Its job is simply to explain the idea well enough that someone thinks:

> “I understand this. That sounds interesting. I might want to participate.”

### What is Builder Season?

Builder Season is a longer, more welcoming evolution of the traditional hackathon.

Instead of asking people to show up for a weekend, race toward a prototype, and compete for a small number of winners, Builder Season gives people a container in which to:

**Commit → Build → Share → Finish → Celebrate**

Participants commit to making something during a defined season, work on it over time, share progress publicly, and finish by putting something real into the world.

“Building” should be interpreted broadly. Depending on the community and season, someone might build software, art, writing, research, educational material, a community project, an event, a performance, an experiment, or something we haven't anticipated.

The builder chooses the scope. The season helps them finish.

### Tone

The page should feel:

* welcoming rather than competitive
* playful and optimistic
* builder-first
* creative
* slightly unconventional
* credible enough to share with established organizations
* concise

Avoid generic startup/AI marketing language.

Do not oversell Builder Season as a revolutionary replacement for hackathons. Present it as an experiment and an evolution of the format.

### Make This Specific to Ada Lovelace Day

This is not a generic Builder Season landing page. It is the landing page for an **Ada Lovelace Builder Season**, so the identity and purpose of this particular season should be visible throughout the page.

Give visitors enough context to understand why Ada Lovelace is being celebrated and why her story remains relevant.

Ada Lovelace represents an unusual combination of mathematical thinking, imagination, creativity, and technology. Her idea of **“poetical science”** provides inspiration for this season: bringing technical and creative ways of thinking together and imagining what might be possible.

The season should also connect Ada's legacy to the larger purpose of **recognizing, encouraging, and making visible the contributions of women in STEM and technology today**.

For the Cardano community, the connection is especially meaningful. Cardano's native currency, ADA, is named for Ada Lovelace. This season is an opportunity to go beyond simply using her name and spend some time exploring who she was, why her work matters, and celebrating women who are building and contributing within Cardano and the wider technology community today.

Participants should be invited to interpret this theme broadly. Their creation might:

* explore Ada Lovelace or her ideas;
* celebrate a woman working in STEM or technology;
* highlight women contributing to Cardano or blockchain;
* explore the idea of “poetical science”;
* combine technology with art, writing, music, education, research, or storytelling;
* encourage girls and women to imagine themselves as scientists, technologists, engineers, mathematicians, artists, inventors, and builders.

Do not make the page feel like a history lesson or a corporate diversity campaign. The purpose is to create an **invitation to make something**.

The visitor should come away thinking:

> Ada's story is interesting. This celebration matters. I have an idea for something I could make.

Keep the Builder Season participation loop at the heart of the page:

**Commit → Build → Share → Finish → Celebrate**

But make the answer to **“Why are we building?”** unmistakably Ada Lovelace.


### Suggested page structure

Use your judgment about the exact copy and visual presentation, but the page should communicate these ideas:

**Hero**

A strong Builder Season headline and very short explanation.

Possible conceptual direction:

> Make something. Finish it. Put it into the world.

Introduce Builder Season as a different kind of hackathon built around sustained making rather than a weekend sprint.

**Why Builder Season?**

Hackathons are great at generating excitement and ideas, but not every builder thrives in a short, competitive sprint.

Builder Season creates more room for learning, experimentation, collaboration, persistence, and actually finishing something.

**How it works**

Communicate the basic loop visually and simply:

**Commit → Build → Share → Finish → Celebrate**

Explain each in only a sentence or short phrase.

**What can you build?**

Make it clear that “builder” does not necessarily mean software developer.

Examples might include:

Software • Art • Writing • Research • Education • Events • Experiments • Media • Open-source contributions

Don't make the list feel exhaustive.

**Build in public**

Explain that participants share progress along the way. The process, learning, setbacks, experiments, and discoveries are part of the season—not just the final polished artifact.

**Finish something**

Emphasize:

> You choose the scope. We ask you to finish it.

Small finished projects are welcome. The goal is bringing an intention into fruition, not demonstrating maximum complexity.

**Celebration**

The season culminates by sharing and celebrating what everyone built. Avoid framing this primarily around judges and winners.

### Call to action

The upcoming season is still being organized.

End with one clear CTA:

**Be notified when the next Builder Season opens.**

Provide a simple email signup/interest form.

If the existing project already has an appropriate form or email integration, reuse it. If not, create the front-end form and clearly identify what still needs to be connected rather than introducing an external service or backend without approval.

### Important constraints

Keep this intentionally small.

Do NOT build:

* participant registration
* authentication
* project submission
* project galleries
* detailed event rules
* judging systems
* reward systems
* dashboards
* season-management infrastructure
* a CMS
* a new design system

Do not invent finalized dates, sponsors, statistics, testimonials, or claims that aren't already supported by the repository.

### Implementation

1. Inspect the repository first.
2. Briefly report what framework and existing conventions you found.
3. Implement the landing page using those conventions.
4. Keep the implementation easy to change as we learn more.
5. Run the existing build/tests or appropriate checks.
6. Report what files you changed and any decisions or assumptions you made.

The goal is a **minimum useful public explanation of Builder Season**, not a finished platform.
